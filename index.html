Job Site Arrival & Check-In Field

Standard Customer Site Arrival Procedure

  1. Secure Proper Parking: Park only in designated, legal, and non-obstructing spaces. Never block a fire lane, Fire Department Connection (FDC), or fire hydrant under any circumstances.
  2. Complete Site Check-In: Locate and check in with the primary site contact immediately upon arrival.
  3. Verify Work Scope & Safety: Confirm the exact scope of work, designated system areas, and all site-specific safety regulations or hazards.
  4. Initiate Fire Alarm Test Mode: If the project involves live fire protection equipment, place the fire alarm system into "Test Mode" with the monitoring station for the estimated duration of the work to prevent false dispatches.
  5. Notify Local Non-Emergency Dispatch: Contact the non-emergency dispatch line for the specific municipality where the work is located. Provide the operator with the following details:
    • Your full name and company name
    • The specific site address and nature of the work
    • The estimated duration of the project
  6. Document Dispatch Confirmation: Request and record the operator’s badge number or confirmation ID in your service notes for tracking and liability records.
Personal Protective Equipment (PPE) Safety

Minimum PPE expected on FDEC job sites.

  • Safety glasses — any overhead work, cutting, grinding, or drilling.
  • Hard hat — active construction sites and any overhead hazard.
  • Cut-resistant gloves — handling pipe, fittings, and sharp edges.
  • Steel or composite toe boots — all field work.
  • High-visibility vest — parking areas, roadways, and construction sites.
  • Hearing protection — sustained exposure to loud equipment.

Inspect PPE before each use. Remove damaged items from service and replace them.

System Impairment & Fire Watch Safety

Governs any work that takes a fire protection system, or part of one, out of service, per National Fire Protection Association (NFPA) 25 impairment procedures.

  1. Notify the building owner or designated representative before the impairment begins.
  2. Determine the extent and expected duration. Plan to restore the system the same day where applicable.
  3. Notify the supervising station (monitoring company) and the Authority Having Jurisdiction (AHJ) where required.
  4. Tag the impaired system and hang the impairment tag at the main control valve.
  5. Verify the customer is aware that no hot work or other ignition sources are active in the protected area.
  6. On completion, test to confirm the system is operational, remove the tag, and notify all parties that protection is restored.
Ladder Safety Safety
  • Inspect the ladder before use. Remove damaged ladders from service.
  • Set extension ladders at the four-to-one (4:1) angle — one (1) foot of base out for every four (4) feet of height.
  • Maintain three (3) points of contact when climbing.
  • Do not stand on the top two (2) rungs or the top cap.
  • Face the ladder. Do not overreach — reposition instead.
  • Use a ladder rated for the combined weight of the worker and tools.
  • For extended overhead work, evaluate whether a scissor or boom lift is the safer option.
Hot Work — Cutting, Welding & Grinding Safety

Applies to any spark- or flame-producing work.

  1. Obtain a hot work permit from the site where one is required.
  2. Clear combustibles within thirty-five (35) feet, or shield them.
  3. Keep a charged fire extinguisher within reach.
  4. Confirm the sprinkler system protecting the area is in service, or provide an approved fire watch.
  5. Maintain a fire watch during the work and for at least thirty (30) minutes afterward.
Lockout / Tagout (LOTO) Safety

Controls hazardous energy before servicing equipment such as fire pumps, controllers, and electrically operated valves.

  1. Identify all energy sources — electrical, pneumatic, hydraulic, and stored water pressure.
  2. Notify affected personnel before shutdown.
  3. Shut down, isolate, lock, and tag each energy source.
  4. Release or relieve stored energy, including trapped water pressure.
  5. Verify zero energy before starting work.
  6. Only the person who applied a lock removes it.
Company Vehicle Operation & Daily Inspection Field
  • Hold a valid driver license and operate only vehicles you are authorized to drive.
  • Complete a walk-around before the first trip each day — tires, lights, fluids, and secured load.
  • Secure all pipe, tools, and compressed gas cylinders before driving.
  • No handheld phone use while driving.
  • Report any accident, damage, or mechanical issue to the office the same day.
  • Keep the vehicle clean and stocked for the next assignment.
Injury & Near-Miss Reporting Safety
  1. Address immediate medical needs first. Call 911 for any serious injury.
  2. Report every injury and every near-miss to your supervisor the same day, no matter how minor.
  3. Do not disturb the scene of a serious incident beyond what is needed to make it safe.
  4. Complete the FDEC incident report while details are fresh.
  5. Near-misses are reported so we can fix the hazard before it causes an injury.
Customer Communication & Conduct Field
  • Represent FDEC professionally in appearance, language, and conduct.
  • Explain what you found and what the work involves in plain terms the customer understands.
  • Route pricing, scope changes, and commitments through the office — do not quote on the spot unless authorized.
  • Document deficiencies clearly with photos so the office can build an accurate scope and quote.
  • Leave the work area as clean as or cleaner than you found it.
End-of-Day Documentation (ServiceTrade) Field
  • Record all work performed, test results, and readings on the correct job and asset.
  • Attach photos of completed work and any deficiencies found.
  • Note every deficiency with enough detail for the office to quote the repair.
  • Capture required signatures before leaving the site.
  • Submit paperwork the same day so inspections and billing are not delayed.

Permit-Required Confined Space Entry Permit

Fire Defense Equipment Company — Quick Hub

Reference guidance and a fillable entry permit for permit-required confined spaces, built on the Occupational Safety and Health Administration (OSHA) standard 29 CFR 1910.146.

Before any entry: confirm the space has been evaluated, the permit is completed and signed by the Entry Supervisor, atmospheric testing is acceptable, and an Attendant is posted. Never enter a permit-required space alone.

Reference

OSHA 1910.146

Is it a confined space?

A confined space meets all three (3):

  • Large enough to enter and perform work
  • Limited or restricted means of entry or exit
  • Not designed for continuous occupancy

FDEC examples: water storage tanks, fire pump pits, below-grade valve vaults, underground main vaults, and tank interiors.

OSHA 1910.146

When is it permit-required?

A confined space is permit-required (PRCS) if it has any one (1) of:

  • A hazardous atmosphere, actual or potential
  • Material that could engulf an entrant
  • Walls that converge inward or a floor that slopes and could trap or asphyxiate
  • Any other recognized serious safety or health hazard

Roles

  • Entry Supervisor — authorizes entry, verifies conditions, cancels the permit.
  • Authorized Entrant — performs the work inside the space.
  • Attendant — stays outside, monitors entrants, never enters, orders evacuation when needed.

Scissor & Boom Lift — Pre-Use Inspection

Fire Defense Equipment Company — Quick Hub

Safety rules and a pre-use inspection checklist for Mobile Elevating Work Platforms (MEWPs) — scissor and boom lifts — per OSHA 29 CFR 1926.453 and the American National Standards Institute (ANSI) A92 series.

Fall protection: in a boom lift, every occupant wears a full-body harness with a lanyard attached to the manufacturer's anchor point at all times. Scissor lifts rely on the guardrail system; follow the manufacturer and FDEC policy on whether a harness is also required. Never climb on or sit on the guardrails.

Key safety rules

OSHA 1926.453

Before you raise the platform

  • Only trained, authorized operators run the lift.
  • Complete the pre-use inspection below each shift.
  • Survey the work area — slope, holes, soft ground, drop-offs, and debris.
  • Look up. Keep at least ten (10) feet from energized power lines up to fifty (50) kilovolts.
  • Set outriggers and wheel chocks where equipped and required.
ANSI A92

While working at height

  • Stay inside the platform with both feet on the deck.
  • Do not exceed the rated load — people, tools, and material combined.
  • Keep the gate or chain closed.
  • Do not use the lift as a crane or to carry suspended loads.
  • Lower the platform before moving, unless the lift is rated to travel elevated.
  • Stop work in high wind, lightning, or other unsafe conditions.
