Recommended study order

  1. Identify components and read gauges — pipe, hangers, bracing, sprinklers, fittings, FDC, hose connections, and supply/system gauges.
  2. Locate and identify control valves, drain valves, and test valves — and confirm the indicator position of each control valve.
  3. Identify system types and water supply — wet, dry, and standpipe systems; fire pumps, tanks, and municipal supply.
  4. Identify alarm and supervisory devices — and verify trouble, supervisory, and alarm signals.
  5. Prepare preliminary documentation — select the correct report, inventory components, and record make, type, and model.
  6. Operate components under direct supervision — exercise control valves, test the water-flow alarm, perform the main drain test, and flush hydrants.

1.1 — Safety

Be ready to explain: personal protective equipment (PPE), lockout/tagout (LOTO), common jobsite hazards, ladder and lift use, and tool condition — and how a system that is being tested can create an impairment.

  • Wear and maintain required PPE, and inspect tools and ground-fault circuit interrupters (GFCI) before use.
  • Recognize mechanical and electrical lockout/tagout, and when it applies to a water-based system task.
  • Recognize hazards such as confined spaces, floor and ceiling openings, poor lighting, ladders, and aerial or scissor lifts.
  • Recognize when work creates a safety or system impairment, and raise it to your supervising technician before proceeding.
1.2 — Identify condition and operation of system components

This is the largest Level I domain. You are expected to reliably locate and identify equipment, read gauges, and recognize damage — without necessarily operating the system.

  • Locate and read supply-side and system-side gauges, and recognize a reading that looks abnormal.
  • Locate and identify control valves, drain valves, and test valves, and confirm the indicator position of each control valve (open versus shut).
  • Visually inspect pipe, hangers, seismic bracing, sprinklers, fittings, the fire department connection (FDC), and hose connections for damage or deficiencies.
  • Identify electronic and mechanical alarm and supervisory devices, and verify trouble, supervisory, and alarm signals.
  • Identify the system type — wet, dry, or standpipe — and the water supply, including fire pumps, tanks, and municipal supply.

Level I mindset: Identification first. Know what the component is, where it belongs, and whether it looks right — then report what you find to a Level II or III technician.

1.3 — Prepare preliminary documentation

Accurate records begin at Level I. You support the inspection by capturing an accurate picture of what is installed.

  • Identify the correct report for the ITM task being performed.
  • Inventory system components and record the size, make, type, and model of sprinklers and valves.
  • Prepare preliminary reports under the direction of a Level II or III technician.
  • Note any damage, missing components, or questionable conditions so they can be evaluated.
1.4 — Operate components under direct supervision

At Level I you perform hands-on tasks while supervised. Know the purpose of each task and the safe sequence before touching the system.

  • Fully exercise control valves — OS&Y valves, post indicator valves (PIVs), wall post indicator valves (WPIVs), and butterfly valves — and return them to the correct position.
  • Test the water-flow alarm, including the water motor gong, pressure switch, and vane-type devices.
  • Perform the main drain test to confirm the water supply is available and the supply path is not obstructed.
  • Flush hydrants, exercise hose connections, and test electronic valve supervisory devices.
  • Before operating anything, coordinate the impairment and notification steps with your supervising technician.
A control valve left in the wrong position after a task can take the system out of service. Always confirm valves are returned to normal and supervisory signals are restored.

Main drain test — pressure-drop calculator

This tool compares the static (no-flow) reading with the residual (full-flow) reading, and against your previous main drain test. It is not a standalone pass/fail determination and does not replace the NFPA 25 procedure.

Enter readings and select Calculate.
How to interpret this comparison

Drop = static − residual. A larger drop than the previous main drain test can indicate a supply-side restriction, such as a partially closed valve or a developing obstruction. A number alone does not establish a cause.

Before concluding anything, confirm the control valves are fully open, the test was taken at the same point under full flow, and the gauge is serviceable — then report the finding to your supervising technician.
